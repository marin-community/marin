## Story in eight sentences

1. Given fixed pretraining data sources, choose their mixture under a training and search compute budget.
2. The regression-based paradigm trains a proxy swarm, fits responses, optimizes their aggregate and validates the selected mixture at larger budgets.
3. Its two decisions are the regression functional form, which governs generalization across mixtures, and the proxy data regime, which governs transfer across scales.
4. Individual affine and log-linear heads cannot represent repetition-induced interior task minima; richer regression families must be judged by their predictions and selected mixtures at affordable swarm sizes.
5. MARINER uses shared nonlinear benefit and harm shapes with bucket-specific amplitudes in the log deficit above a fitted floor.
6. Simulated epoching matches target repetition through subset sizes; a static cap instead restricts allowed weights, and neither matching nor a cap establishes target-optimal transfer by itself.
7. The current 39-bucket results support improved held-out ranks and lower measured losses than proportional/UniMax at two compute budgets; conventional nonlinear and matched-trained-Olmix comparisons remain explicit placeholders.
8. Component trade-offs, joint-support failures, fixed-subset uncertainty and residual scale dependence delimit those claims.

### B1. Fixed-bucket, single-phase data mixing

- [Problem formulation opening (user, 2026-09-06, following Percy's story template; paper §3.1 first paragraph, before the notation): P = choose the mixture of a pretraining run before it is trained, i.e. given buckets and a target (N_tgt, D_tgt) find the mixture minimizing the run's evaluation objective using proxy runs whose total compute is a small fraction of the target's. R = the mixture is one of the few choices that changes model quality at fixed compute, and the target run is too expensive to repeat per candidate. C1 = one free coordinate per bucket, so an affordable swarm samples the simplex sparsely. C2 = small buckets are repeated at the target budget, so a task's response to a bucket's weight rises then falls and depends on the budget. C3 = proxy runs are small and evaluated once, so on many tasks the swarm's variation is within run-to-run noise. Previous approaches = parametric surrogates fitted to small swarms handle C1 but take weight as the coordinate, fit monotone responses, and train proxies that never repeat data, so repetition is absent from their data and their model. The approach, key innovation (epoch-matched swarm + epoch-aware surrogate + noise screen), intuition, and quantitative results are for the Introduction, not Background.]

- Let the training corpus be partitioned upstream into \(M\) fixed buckets, \(\mathcal B_1,\ldots,\mathcal B_M\), with full pool sizes \(P_i=|\mathcal B_i|\).
- A training run has model size \(N\), token budget \(D\), and mixture \(w\in\mathcal W_D\subseteq\Delta^{M-1}\). The optimizer chooses \(w\), but cannot change the buckets or add data.
- The sampler allocates approximately \(D w_i\) token positions to bucket \(i\), then samples examples according to the bucket's operational sampling rule.
- The mixture \(w\) therefore induces a stochastic policy over the selected training multiset, its order, and the resulting trained model.
- For atomic evaluation task \(t\), define \(f_t(w;N,D)\) as the expected validation loss after marginalizing over data sampling, ordering, optimization, and evaluation noise. A macro objective combines tasks as \(F(w;N,D)=\sum_t a_t f_t(w;N,D)\), where \(a_t\geq0\), \(\sum_ta_t=1\), and lower values are better.
- For target configuration \((N_{\mathrm{tgt}},D_{\mathrm{tgt}})\), the desired mixture is \(w_{\mathrm{tgt}}^*=\arg\min_{w\in\mathcal W_{D_{\mathrm{tgt}}}}F(w;N_{\mathrm{tgt}},D_{\mathrm{tgt}})\).
- The response surface and its optimum depend on both \(N\) and \(D\); changing \(D\) also changes how often finite buckets are repeated.

### B2. Offline surrogate optimization

- Offline methods train \(R\) smaller proxy runs. Run \(r\) uses \((N_r,D_r,w_r)\) and produces observations \(y_{r,t}\), giving supervised data \(\{(w_r,y_{r,t})\}_{r=1}^R\) for each task-level surrogate.
- The fitted surrogate \(\hat f_t(w)\) predicts each task response over the epoch-matched proxy design, and the corresponding macro surrogate \(\hat F(w)=\sum_t a_t\hat f_t(w)\) is optimized over the target-feasible mixture set.
- The predicted optimum is a proposal rather than a result; it must be materialized in a new training run and compared with baselines under fresh randomness.
- A finer partition makes the mixture more expressive, but every added bucket is another free coordinate of the simplex and the runs needed to cover it at fixed resolution grow exponentially with the number of buckets, while the swarm budget does not; a practical swarm of a few hundred runs leaves most candidate mixtures far from any observed one. [Paper states this generally; the 39-bucket/38-coordinate instance belongs to E1/M2.]
- This sparse high-dimensional setting makes the surrogate's structural assumptions central to both extrapolation and optimum selection.
- Predictive error over typical held-out mixtures is insufficient by itself; a useful surrogate must also rank strong mixtures and select low-regret optima near sparsely sampled boundaries.

### B3. Configuring proxy runs and the swarm

- The swarm compute budget is the central quantity (user, 2026-09-06): given \(C_{\mathrm{swarm}}\), choose \(N_r\), \(D_r\), \(R\) with \(R\,C(N_r,D_r)\approx C_{\mathrm{swarm}}\). The three compete: larger \(N_r\) = closer stand-in for the target and more tasks above their noise floor; larger \(D_r\) = closer to the target's training regime; larger \(R\) = denser coverage of a mixture space whose dimension is set by the number of buckets. - Mixture proportion alone does not describe how often a finite bucket is repeated.
- At mixture \(w\), the target run's materialized epoch count for bucket \(i\) is

  \[
  E_i^{\mathrm{tgt}}(w)\approx\frac{D_{\mathrm{tgt}}w_i}{P_i}.
  \]

- Proxy run \(r\) instead materializes \(\mathcal S_{r,i}\subseteq\mathcal B_i\), with \(S_{r,i}=|\mathcal S_{r,i}|\), and has materialized epoch count

  \[
  E_{r,i}(w)\approx\frac{D_rw_i}{S_{r,i}}.
  \]

- Simulated epoching chooses each proxy subset so that

  \[
  \frac{D_r}{S_{r,i}}\approx\frac{D_{\mathrm{tgt}}}{P_i},
  \qquad
  E_{r,i}(w)\approx E_i^{\mathrm{tgt}}(w)
  \]

  for every candidate mixture \(w\). Each proxy therefore reproduces the per-bucket repetition that the target-budget run would encounter at the same mixture.
- The same weight can correspond to negligible repetition for a large bucket and many passes over a small bucket.
- Learning from new examples and repeating already-seen examples need not have the same marginal value, so the response can depend on materialized epochs in ways that a proportion-only model cannot share across buckets or budgets.
- Small proxy runs would rarely repeat the full target corpus enough to expose this behavior.
- [Held et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11747) and [Zhou et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07597) likewise use repetition-controlled subsampling to emulate a larger target budget. [2026-09-08, Codex, at Calvin's request: Zhou et al. is now cited directly where Background introduces the simulated-epoching construction, immediately before Equation `eq:simulated-epoching`; the existing Related Work citation remains.]
- Epoch matching does not make proxy and target models equivalent; it isolates repetition while \(N_r\), \(D_r\), optimization dynamics, and measurement quality remain scale-dependent.

### B6. Why monotone response heads are insufficient

- B5 gives the surrogate two requirements: express exposure in materialized epochs, and allow each task response to become non-monotone as repetition increases.
- Nonparametric regressors can represent non-monotone response surfaces, but their sample requirements grow poorly with dimension.
- Parametric laws such as [DML](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16952), [BiMix](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14908), and [CAMEL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08022) build from monotone log-linear or power-law components; their aggregates need not be monotone, but they do not parameterize repetition under a finite data pool.
- Olmix is the closest operational comparator for our many-bucket setting and was used in the [OLMo 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13961) pipeline; it models each atomic task as \(\hat f_t(w)=c_t+\exp(\beta_t^\top w)\). Along every straight segment in the mixture simplex, the head is monotone or flat and cannot represent a strict interior task minimum. If a population response at an interior point is at least \(d\) below both endpoints, every monotone approximation has absolute error at least \(d/2\) at one of the three points (Appendix proposition `prop:monotone-gap`). This is a limitation of each task head, not of an aggregate of heads with opposing slopes; a dip in noisy observations alone does not establish the population premise.
- This form is appropriate when repetition is negligible, as in the Olmix proxy experiments, but B5 shows that this regime can hide the interior optimum created by finite target data.

### M3. Compute-budgeted swarm design

- Current run-allocation rule: \(R_{\mathrm{fit}}=\lfloor C_{\mathrm{swarm}}/C(N_r,D_r)\rfloor-n_{\mathrm{rep}}\), with \(R_{\mathrm{fit}}\ge2M+5\) a retrospective starting recommendation. This counts nominal response parameters and distinct mixtures; at M=39 there are 83 response parameters (78 amplitudes, an intercept, three shared shapes, and one floor multiplier). The ridge tuning coefficient is excluded; counting it would give 84 fitted/tuned quantities, a different convention that is not used for this rule. Repeats are additional. It requires exposure coverage and does not guarantee identifiability. The fixed 39-bucket study does not validate this rule at other bucket counts. Olmix recommends \(3(M+1)\).
- Identification requires variation that distinguishes centered benefit and harm columns, not only enough rows (Appendix `app:tail-identification`). A constant column is confounded with the free intercept; positive ridge selects zero for that amplitude, whereas unpenalized NNLS need not identify it uniquely. Dependence and near dependence remain problems when columns vary. Softplus harm is nonzero and varying below its threshold, so failure to cross the threshold does not force a zero harm amplitude.


- We allocate a total budget \(C_{\mathrm{swarm}}=\sum_r C(N_r,D_r)\) between the number of proxy runs and the compute of each run. Subject to sufficient mixture coverage, we use the largest practical proxy model and keep \(D_r/N_r\) close to \(D_{\mathrm{tgt}}/N_{\mathrm{tgt}}\) when feasible; if model size is hardware-limited, we allocate the remaining compute to longer runs or additional mixtures while preserving epoch matching.
- Design choices moved from B5 (Calvin's framing, 2026-09-06, clarified 2026-09-08): estimating scale dependence along fixed-TPP tracks consumes compute that could cover more mixtures. We therefore use one proxy configuration and spend the budget on one swarm, aiming to match target tokens per parameter, as [Li et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14071) recommend. The remaining optimum drift makes this an approximation; the choice is a practical allocation under a limited budget, not evidence that scale dependence is absent or unimportant.
- If matching the target's TPP at the chosen per-run budget would need a proxy too large for abundantly available accelerator instances, use the largest proxy that fits and train it longer: cap \(N_r\), raise \(D_r\), and enlarge each \(S_{r,i}\) so that \(D_r/S_{r,i}\approx D_{\mathrm{tgt}}/P_i\) still holds. This moves the token budget closer to the target's and may reduce sensitivity to an unrepresentative materialized subset. The latter is a design rationale, not a measured reduction in subset-resampling variance.
- Proxy size must still support informative comparisons on the target tasks. Arbitrarily shrinking the model to afford longer runs can leave harder-task differences too small relative to training noise to guide mixture selection. Adequate task signal constrains the allocation; it does not by itself imply that the proxy must be compute-optimal.

### M5. Parametric surrogate model

- Structural interpretation (Appendix `app:surrogate-properties`): with \(\delta_t=\hat f_t-\phi_t\) and \(\eta_{ti}=-\alpha_{t,i}b+\beta_{t,i}h\), bucket effects multiply the deficit: \(\delta_t=e^{c_t}\prod_i e^{\eta_{ti}}\ge e^{c_t-\sum_i\alpha_{t,i}}>0\). A log-deficit improvement \(B>0\) removes the fraction \(1-e^{-B}\) of the remaining deficit. Equal epoch increments need not yield equal log-deficit improvements. The finite fitted response cannot reach its offset \(\phi_t\); this is no bound on actual attainable loss, and does not derive the exponential law from training dynamics.
- Turnover is conditional: positive benefit and harm amplitudes guarantee a finite positive bucket-response minimum on the unbounded exposure domain when \(0<\kappa<1\); at \(\kappa=1\), require \(\alpha\rho>2\beta\operatorname{softplus}(-\tau)\sigma(-\tau)\). The fitted grid excludes \(\kappa>1\). Neither uniqueness, a minimum in the feasible range \(E_i\le D_{\mathrm{tgt}}/P_i\), nor its attainability under simplex reallocation follows. The infinite initial benefit slope for \(\kappa<1\) is a modeling assumption.

- For each retained component, we fit an \(O(M)\)-parameter response surface in materialized-epoch space that can represent non-monotone repetition effects.
- We select the model family using the expanded Delphi development registry, then freeze it and use the canonical 280-run panel for the final apples-to-apples comparison with Olmix and the other baselines.

- Equation (paper §4.3, from `single_phase_observatory_models_20260902.py` + `single_phase_observatory_registry_20260902.py`, final model `weibull_softplus_unscaled@kappa_floor_link_flat15`, decided 2026-09-07): \(\hat f_t(w)=\phi_t+\exp(\eta_t(w))\), \(\eta_t(w)=c_t-\sum_i\alpha_{t,i}\,b(E_i(w))+\sum_i\beta_{t,i}\,h(E_i(w))\) with \(\alpha,\beta\ge0\), \(b(E)=1-\exp(-(\rho E)^\kappa)\) (Weibull saturation, shared rate and power), \(h(E)=\mathrm{softplus}(\log(1+E)-\tau)^2\) (shared-threshold log-quadratic overexposure, per bucket), free intercept. Parameters: \(2M\) nonnegative amplitudes + intercept + 3 shared shape parameters + one floor multiplier per task. Exposures are the target's materialized epochs \(E_i(w)=D_{\mathrm{tgt}}w_i/P_i\) (equal to the proxy's under simulated epoching; pool-fraction rows divide by the fraction).

### M6. Mixture optimization

- Marginal conditions (Appendix `app:marginal-conditions`): with \(s_i=D_{\mathrm{tgt}}/P_i\), finite marginals of the fixed aggregate satisfy \(g_i=s_i\sum_t a_t\delta_t\eta'_{ti}(E_i)\), equal to one multiplier on active buckets and no smaller on excluded buckets at a continuous local minimum. For one task, minimize the log deficit and use \(s_i\eta'_{ti}\). These necessary conditions explain the units and the deficit weighting; they do not certify global optimality, interiority, or moderate repetition. Fixed floor offsets cancel from the optimized objective, while the chosen floors affect its fitted responses.
- A zero-weight bucket with positive benefit amplitude on any positively weighted task with \(\kappa_t<1\) has a negative infinite one-sided slope and cannot be excluded at an exact continuous local minimum: an order-\(\epsilon^{\kappa_t}\) gain dominates the order-\(\epsilon\) reallocation cost. Keep this separate from finite-derivative KKT conditions. Numerical tolerances and sampler rounding can produce zero weights.

- We fix the component aggregation weights \(a_t\) and solve

  \[
  \hat w_{\mathrm{tgt}}^*=\arg\min_{w\in\mathcal W_{D_{\mathrm{tgt}}}}\sum_t a_t\hat f_t(w).
  \]

- The sum is the surrogate's prediction of the aggregate exactly when the aggregate is linear in the component responses, which holds for both of our targets: bits per byte pooled over the Uncheatable components is total bits over total bytes, so it equals \(\sum_t a_t\,\mathrm{BPB}_t\) with \(a_t\) the byte share of component \(t\) (0.17, 0.15, 0.17, 0.12, 0.15, 0.15, 0.09 for AO3, arXiv CS, arXiv physics, BBC News, GitHub C++, GitHub Python, Wikipedia), and the Table-9 macro is the unweighted mean of its 51 atomic components, \(a_t=1/51\), so a task with seven subtasks contributes seven terms. The weights are properties of the evaluation sets and do not depend on \(w\). For a non-linear aggregate (accuracy-based macros, geometric means, ranks) the same expression is a plug-in approximation rather than the aggregate's prediction.
- The \(a_t\) are evaluation weights, not reliability weights: a noisy component with a large byte share steers the optimum through its noisy fit, and the reliability screen of M4 excludes components rather than down-weighting them. Figure R7 is the justification: on every retained component the fit error is a constant fraction of the swarm spread and run noise is a small share of it, so down-weighting by noise would not change what limits the fit.
- If the screen removes a component, it leaves both the objective and the reported aggregate, with the remaining \(a_t\) renormalized; renormalizing over a subset does not change the argmin, which is what the three-component optimization of Table R-objectives does deliberately. At Qwen3 360M/1.6B the objectives in Results are the full aggregates: Table 9 keeps all 51 components by policy (a known eval), and the Uncheatable screen removed none.
- The reported feasible set is the mixture simplex without an epoch cap or KL penalty. Fixed pools and token budget still imply \(E_i\le D_{\mathrm{tgt}}/P_i\). Caps are sensitivity checks: the uncapped policies match the former cap-6 Uncheatable and cap-8 Table-9 policies (maximum 5.25 / 7.50 epochs), while cap-6 Table 9 remains a distinct sensitivity row.
- We materialize the selected mixture in fresh runs and compare it with predeclared baselines under matched training conditions.

- Solver (paper §4.4 last paragraph, from `optimize_delphi_matched_policies_20260906.py` and `materialize_delphi_link_validation_20260906.py`, frozen 2026-09-07): the aggregate \(\sum_t a_t(\phi_t+\exp\eta_t(w))\) is not separable; SLSQP, a local method whose only guard against local minima is the multistart (maxiter 500, ftol 1e-11), from five starts (the proportional mixture and four swarm mixtures drawn with seed 20260906, each projected into the feasible set), a polish pass with a coarser finite-difference step (1e-6), best feasible endpoint kept (endpoint spread recorded in `restart_diagnostics.csv`); the continuous optimum is rounded to the sampler's block grid (`grid.MIXTURE_BLOCK_SIZE`) by constrained prefix counts and refined by an exchange search that may not raise the objective. The current proposal uses no epoch cap or KL penalty; the fitted floors restrict predicted gains, not actual attainable loss. The following KL experiment used an earlier unbounded-floor model. An Olmix-style KL 0.05 penalty was tested as a preregistered comparison on the unbounded-κ model's optima (Calvin's hypothesis: worse), `dm-delphi-3e18-lwspu-kappafloor-kl05-v6e8-20260907`, measured 2026-09-07: Uncheatable 0.9916 vs 0.9890 unpenalized; Table 9 cap 6 1.0799 vs 1.0613, cap 8 1.0860 vs 1.0672 (Olmix's best 1.0769, Olmix at KL 0.05 1.0814). The penalized mixtures sit at TV 0.21–0.22 (Table 9) and 0.43 (Uncheatable) from the unpenalized optima; on Table 9 the penalty moved 5.6 points out of synthetic QA, 5.3 out of Stack-Edu (+FIM) and 3.4 out of high-quality literature into CC-HQ (+7.3) and finance/entertainment/health web; the components that got worse are the ones those buckets feed (basic-skills arithmetic +0.16, sciq +0.07, basic-skills coding +0.07, jeopardy +0.07, drop, mbpp, bash), while squad, naturalqs, socialiqa and logical reasoning improved. Note the materializer's 'predicted' value for a KL run is the penalized objective (0.9953 / 1.0876 / 1.0875); the surrogate's own predictions were 0.9852 / 1.0711 / 1.0710, and the final flat-profile model predicts 0.9881 / 1.0713 / 1.0713. Paper §6.1 sentence (2026-09-07): the penalty does part of Olmix's work and hurts ours on both objectives.
- Historical cap validation (superseded as the default by uncapped optimization): 6 for Uncheatable and 6 / 8 for Table 9 (policy constraints, not fitted). Rationale: the measured cap sweeps of the additive models (DSP 2026-08-28 and 2026-09-01, MARINER 2026-09-02) turn around past 6–8 epochs while their predictions keep improving (over-extrapolation where the swarm has no support), and the optimizer stops using the cap near 6 (Uncheatable) and 8 (Table 9); cap 6 matches the largest cap the baselines were measured at, cap 8 is Table 9's converged optimum, Olmix's own cap is 4. It is 0 at SNR 1, 0.75 at SNR 2, 0.89 at SNR 3 and above 0.96 from SNR 5. The distance from a point up to the curve is variance a better model could still explain; the distance from the curve to 1 is what only more repeats could recover.

The results concern the fixed fitted response family and do not establish predictive accuracy, transfer, sample complexity, or the exponential mixing law. Data Mixing Laws motivates that law empirically; Olmix's reuse theory assumes it.]

- `prop:monotone-gap` (B6): every log-linear task head is monotone along every simplex segment. A population interior dip of depth at least \(d\) forces error of at least \(d/2\) for any monotone approximation at an endpoint or the interior point. Aggregated heads need not be monotone, and noisy dips do not establish the premise.
- `prop:coordinate-invariance` (Results ablations): invertible diagonal rescaling \(E=Sw\) preserves unrestricted linear-projection function classes, including OLS and Olmix. Equivalent fitting requires a transformed penalty and treatment of ties, and equivalent mixture optimization uses \(S\Delta\). MARINER's fixed shared nonlinear shape grid generally breaks this invariance for unequal bucket scales.
- `lem:deficit-bounds` (M5): factorization gives \(\delta_t\ge\exp(c_t-\sum_i\alpha_{t,i})>0\). Benefit multipliers lie in \([e^{-\alpha_{t,i}},1]\), harm multipliers are at least one, and their product may lie on either side of one. The finite fitted response cannot reach \(\phi_t\), but measured loss can be lower. Fractional gains are a one-line identity; since \(h(0)>0\), a zero-exposure comparison uses differences of bucket effects.
- `prop:turnover` (M5): positive amplitudes and \(0<\kappa<1\), or \(\kappa=1\) with the explicit derivative condition, give an interior global bucket-response minimum on \([0,\infty)\). This does not prove uniqueness or feasibility within \([0,D_{\mathrm{tgt}}/P_i]\).
- `app:marginal-conditions` (M6): finite simplex marginals are equal on active buckets and no better for excluded buckets, with task weights \(a_t\delta_t\). Handle the \(\kappa<1\) zero-weight singularity separately: a positive benefit on a positively weighted task prevents exact exclusion in continuous optimization. This first-sliver preference is an assumption of the family; rounding and numerical tolerances remain relevant.
- `lem:curvature` (M5; added 2026-09-08 by CC after Calvin asked whether b is concave and h convex): for κ ≤ 1 the Weibull benefit is concave on E > 0 (b' is a product of nonincreasing positive factors; κ > 1 would give an S-shape, excluded by the grid). The harm is convex in log(1+E) everywhere but in epochs only below the inflection E_τ = (e−1)e^τ − 1, since h''(E) = 2σ(z)²(1 − softplus(z))/(1+E)² with z = log(1+E) − τ (numerically verified); beyond E_τ it is concave and grows like (log E)². E_τ = 3.7 / 11.7 / 33.5 / 93 / 254 / 692 epochs for τ = 1..6; twelve of the 58 frozen fits sit at τ = 1, where the proposals' most repeated buckets (5.3 and 7.5 epochs) are past the inflection. Consequences: η_t and the aggregate are convex in w on the region where all active exposures are below E_τ (E linear in w, −b convex, h convex, exp convex increasing), so the optimizer's local-solution caveat is needed only because of the concave harm tail; turnover (unbounded h) and the super-polynomial loss growth are unaffected. Interpretation: a shared τ is a common critical repetition count e^τ − 1 (1.7, 6.4, 19, ... epochs). Not yet tested empirically: the harm growth law itself (squared softplus in E, or unsquared softplus in log-epochs) as Table 10 rows.
- `app:tail-identification` (M3 and Results coverage): the precise asymptotic multiplier is \(e^{-\alpha+\beta\tau^2}E^{\beta\log E-2\beta\tau}\), with the ratio tending to one as \(E\to\infty\). Positive harm permits growth faster than every fixed power, although the feasible exposure interval is bounded. Identification requires informative centered design columns; ridge can select a coefficient without making it data-identified. A soft threshold does not make subthreshold harm identically zero, and threshold non-crossing does not force NNLS to return zero.
