# Final review

## 1. What I verified as correct

**Workstream A.** Every number in `report.md` reproduces from `aggregate_metrics.csv` and `corrected_contrasts.csv` (spot-checked all 18 aggregate rows and all 8 contrasts). The Nadeau–Bengio arithmetic is right: `factor = 1/(K·R) + 1/(K−1) = 1/25 + 1/4`, `t₀.₉₇₅,₂₄ = 2.0639`, and e.g. `−0.178197 − 2.0639×0.030169 = −0.240463` matches `ci_low` exactly.

Leakage control is sound. `select_shape` clusters only `pool.weights[train]` and tunes against `macro[fit_rows]`/`macro[test_rows]` with both strictly inside the outer training split; `response_floor` and the column normalization in `fit_head` are computed on training outcomes only; `permuted_exposures` derives from token inventory, not outcomes. The `dsp_outcome_permutation` control shuffles training labels only and collapses to roughly the constant model (0.3457/0.2769 vs 0.3605/0.2929), which is the right behavior for a pipeline that isn't leaking.

I specifically checked the repeated-fold degeneracy risk, since `block_labels` uses `n_init=50` and could return the same K-means optimum for every seed. It does not: dclm fold sizes are (65,49,70,123,56) for repeat 0, (64,110,71,71,47) for repeat 1, (42,70,49,89,113) for repeat 2. The five repeats are genuinely distinct partitions, so the R=5 term in the correction is earned.

**Workstream B.** The identification figures in the report match `identification.json` (0.45564→0.456, 0.018715→0.019, 23.913→23.91, 7.839→7.84). `mechanism_gate.json` shows all four conditions false and the verdict string matches. The report's claim that "the nonlinear shape, one-epoch exhaustion threshold, ridge, and quality shrinkage are fixed across variants" checks out — `repeated_damage(r) = r/(105+r)` is exactly `canonical.damage(r+1, 1.0)`, so the state and cumulative variants share one damage shape and differ only in how the excess-over-one-epoch quantity is formed, which is the mechanism under test. Sign conventions are correct throughout: features enter as `[−benefit, damage]` with coefficients bounded nonnegative, so benefit lowers and damage raises the loss.

## 2. Blocking defects

**A-1. The inventory-scramble gate does not test inventory for the promoted head, and it contradicts the report's own interpretation rule.**

`features()` returns `(log1p(exposure), positive=False)` for `linear_epoch_log_link` but `(−(1−exp(−rate·x)), positive=True)` for `dsp_permuted_inventory` (`benchmark_olmix_swarm_single_phase_dsp_20260901.py:173-190`). The contrast `linear_epoch_log_link_minus_dsp_permuted_inventory` therefore varies three things at once — feature transform, sign constraint, and inventory correspondence — plus a fourth, tuning budget (6 candidate shapes vs 24, since only `dsp_`-prefixed variants get `RATE_GRID`).

The report's own stated rule calls for the matched pair: "the inventory-correct DSP beats both the raw-weight baseline **and its** inventory-permuted ablation." The permuted ablation *of* `linear_epoch_log_link` would be `log1p(permuted_exposure)`, and it was never run. The gate implements the unmatched contrast and writes `"dclm_10k.rmse_beats_inventory_scramble": true` into `promotion_gate.json`, a durable artifact that will be read at face value later.

This matters most in `high_quality_10k`, where `ci_high = −0.002696` — the interval clears zero by 12% of one corrected SE. A confound of that size is entirely capable of accounting for the whole margin.

The matched contrast does exist in the results and favors the mechanism (`dsp_benefit` 0.14903 vs `dsp_permuted` 0.21777 in dclm; 0.11246 vs 0.16655 in hq; regret 0.0178 vs 0.1081 and 0.0528 vs 0.1230). So the fix is cheap — but as written the report claims a controlled inventory test it did not run for the model it is promoting.

**B-1. "It does not beat tied abstention" is unsatisfiable on this panel and cannot be cited as evidence.**

`tied_abstention_regret` is `0.00000` for every variant in the results table, and I confirmed it is 0.0 in every raw fold row I read. Tied continuation is optimal for every confirmatory state in every fold. Meanwhile `tied_decision_regret` is a mean of `candidates_actual[selected] − candidates_actual.min()`, which is nonnegative by construction (`benchmark_delphi_crossed_prefix_data_exhaustion_20260901.py:241`).

So the gate condition `beats_tied_abstention` reduces to `mean(nonnegative) < 0` — impossible. No model on this panel could ever satisfy it. The verdict nonetheless lists its failure as one of three reasons against promotion: "its corrected interval overlaps zero, **it does not beat tied abstention**, and leave-one-prefix-out transfer is worse." The other two reasons are real; this one carries zero information and reads to a non-specialist as independent corroborating evidence.

## 3. Nonblocking limitations

**Workstream A**

- *Incumbent asymmetry, undisclosed.* `fit_olmix_loglinear_model` is an unregularized 118-parameter nonconvex Huber fit (δ=0.02, 48 starts) with no inner CV; `candidate_shapes` gives it the single degenerate `Shape(0,0,0)`. Challengers get nested ridge/floor/rate selection plus 42-task fitting. This is faithful to the incumbent *as specified*, and is the right comparison for "should we replace it" — but the report should say plainly that the contrast measures "epoch-exposure features **plus** a regularized estimator" against the incumbent as specified, not the feature change alone.
- *The ablation meant to break that confound is broken.* `raw_weight_ridge_log_link` selects `ridge=3.0` — the grid maximum — in literally every fold I inspected (16/16 rows read), with `median_selected_ridge = 3.0` in both pools, and diverges numerically (dclm RMSE 142.29 with repeat SD **305.48**; individual folds hit 1568.3). Its poor showing reflects grid censoring as much as feature inadequacy, so the estimator-vs-feature decomposition is not actually established in either direction. The report's "beats the raw-weight baseline" leg is uninformative as run.
- *The ridge grid does not bracket the optimum for the promoted candidate either.* `median_selected_ridge` for `linear_epoch_log_link` is 3.0 (grid max) in dclm and 0.03 (grid min) in hq. The grid `(0.03, 0.3, 3.0)` binds at opposite ends in the two pools, and binds differently across arms — the permuted control pegs at 0.03 in both. Widening the grid is cheap and would make the ablation comparisons interpretable.
- *Single permutation draw.* `permuted_exposures` uses one fixed `default_rng(FOLD_SEED)` permutation reused across all 25 folds. The corrected interval treats the permuted arm as fixed and never integrates over draw-to-draw variability. With the hq margin at −0.0027, this is a live fragility.
- *"Nearby mixtures do not leak across a reported fold" overstates K-means.* K-means minimizes within-cluster variance; it provides no margin guarantee, and boundary mixtures in adjacent folds can be arbitrarily close. It reduces leakage; it does not preclude it.
- *Two of four gate conditions have no uncertainty at all.* The selection-regret legs are bare point comparisons of a heavy-tailed statistic (per-fold values range 0 to 1.08). They happen to be robust here — the linear head's per-fold regret is consistently at or near 0 while the incumbent's is not — but the gate doesn't show that.
- *Tuning/estimator mismatch.* `select_shape` tunes every variant against the scalar macro, while the final DSP fit uses all 42 tasks. Applied uniformly and mildly conservative for the challengers, but worth a line.
- *"Adding repetition damage hurts" is a numerical statement in hq.* `dsp_benefit_damage_log_link` posts 20.66 with repeat SD 38.9 — a 236-column design on ~290 rows. In dclm (0.2287) the claim is a clean statistical one; in hq it is divergence.
- *"Two independent swarms"* is fair for design and pool (different bucket sets, 118 vs 120) but the 42 tasks and training recipe are shared, so residual correlation between the two replications remains.

**Workstream B**

- *`state_exhaustion` is exactly `blend_1p00`, and this changes how B's question 2 should be answered.* At w=1 the blend branch reduces term-for-term to the `state_exhaustion` else-branch; at w=0 it reduces to `cumulative`. RMSE along the path is 0.01762 → 0.01732 → 0.01697 → 0.01663 → **0.01845**. Smooth ~0.00033-per-step decline, then a reversal at the endpoint five times larger and opposite in sign. That pattern argues the blend's gain is shrinkage toward the well-conditioned cumulative feature rather than partial exhaustion — precisely the "flexible interpolation" reading. The report never states the endpoint identity, so this evidence is invisible to a reader.
- *The blend's t-statistic is invariant along the path.* mean/SE is −1.010, −1.022, −0.948 (confirmatory) and −1.138, −1.171, −1.076 (without cap4). Effect and uncertainty scale together with w, so **no** blend weight in this family can reach |t| = 2.06 on this panel. Worth stating, because it converts "unresolved" into a quantified power statement.
- *The repeated-damage block is essentially unidentified, and the report doesn't say so.* Residual SD fraction 0.019 after phase-1 exposure and state effects, effective rank 7.84 of 39. The Identification section reports the number without interpretation, and B's question 1 asks exactly this. A reader could plausibly misread 0.019 as favorable. The counterpoint `repeat_bucket_residual_fraction_ge_0p2 = 22` was computed and stored but omitted from the report.
- *`fresh_share` is bimodal* (q05=0.0, q25=0.0, median=1.0, q95=1.0) — the fresh/repeat split behaves as a near-binary per-bucket switch, not a graded quantity. Computed, not reported; it explains both the identification difficulty and the poor transfer.
- *LOPO anchoring is undescribed.* `leave_one_prefix_out` sets the held prefix's intercept from that prefix's observed tied outcome, so `predicted = tied_outcome[held] + (tensor − tied_feature)·β`. This is a good, decision-relevant test of coefficient transfer given one anchor — but it is not a fully held-out prefix, and the report should say which it is.
- *`constant_exhaustion` dominates every decision and transfer metric while losing RMSE* — best Spearman (0.624), lowest tied decision regret (0.00011), best LOPO (0.008627, 1.8× better than cumulative and 4.7× better than `state_exhaustion`). This deserves a sentence, with the caveat that its regret advantage comes mostly from abstaining rather than selecting better (`untied_selection_fraction` is 0.0 in most folds).
- *B's repeats are near-duplicates.* `action_fold_ids` varies only its first center via `argmax(features @ normal)`; the remaining four are deterministic farthest-point picks. Several fold statistics recur exactly across repeats — `tied_decision_regret` 0.0506836573282877 at (r0,f3) and (r1,f1); 0.013447374105453491 at (r0,f3), (r1,f1), (r2,f0) — so the R=5 term in the correction is partly unearned and the true SEs are up to ~1.25× larger. For B this is conservative (wider intervals cannot rescue a conclusion of "overlaps zero"), but the same code path would matter if it were ever reused to support a positive claim.
- *No inner tuning in B at all.* `RIDGE` and `QUALITY_SHRINKAGE` are fixed constants inherited from the prior benchmark's scaling. This is the right call for an equal-parameter comparison and is honestly described; noting only for completeness.

## 4. Answers to the posed questions

**A1 — Is `olmix_exact_macro` a fair reproduction?** Yes as a *reproduction* — it is the incumbent's own law, loss, δ, and 48-start procedure, fit on the same folds to the same macro target and scored identically. But it is not a *matched* comparator: it is unregularized and untuned against challengers that get nested ridge/floor/rate selection and 42-task ensembling. The report presents the gap as a feature win without disclosing the estimator gap. The one design element that would have separated them — `raw_weight_ridge_log_link`, correctly matched on sign constraint and tuning grid — is pegged at the ridge-grid maximum in every fold and divergent in dclm, so the decomposition is unresolved. Note the partial defense that does survive: in hq the regularized 42-task estimator on raw weights (0.29259) is *worse* than the incumbent (0.22888) and no better than a constant (0.29288), which is evidence the estimator alone isn't what's winning. That argument is weakened, not destroyed, by the grid pegging.

**A2 — Do the fold geometry, nested tuning, and corrected intervals rule out leakage or a favorable split?** Leakage: yes, convincingly. Tuning is strictly nested, floors and scales are training-only, and the outcome-permutation control lands at the constant baseline. Favorable-split artifact: mostly. The five repeats are genuinely distinct partitions, so the correction's R term is earned, and the effect sizes against the incumbent (−0.178, −0.111) are 4–6 corrected SEs from zero and survive even the most pessimistic re-weighting of the correlation between repeats. Selection regret is directionally consistent fold by fold. The one exception is the hq inventory-scramble contrast, which does *not* survive a conservative treatment of repeat correlation (recomputing with R=1 pushes `ci_high` to roughly +0.008). The "nearby mixtures do not leak" sentence is stronger than K-means supports.

**A3 — Does the permutation identify epoch exposure?** Partially, and less than the report implies. The permutation is a real test only because the features are nonlinear — column normalization in `fit_head` makes a per-bucket rescale a no-op under a linear link, so what is being tested is whether the *inventory-indexed curvature* is assigned to the right buckets. That is narrower than "epoch exposure is the mechanism": any bucket-size-correlated nonlinear warping would pass. Within the matched saturating family the result is clean and consistent across both pools on both RMSE and regret. But the contrast the report and gate actually publish is the unmatched one (Blocking A-1), and it rests on a single permutation draw. So: the gain is not generic capacity — `dsp_permuted_inventory` has identical model size — but "generic regularization" cannot yet be excluded, because the ablation designed to exclude it is grid-censored.

**A4 — Is promoting the linear head defensible, and what can be claimed?**

Promoting it *to fresh prospective validation* is defensible. That is a low bar and the evidence clears it: large, consistent, out-of-fold gains in two independently designed swarms, a working negative control, and a simpler functional form that is statistically indistinguishable from the saturating alternative (CIs straddle zero in both pools) while achieving 10× lower selection regret in hq.

Claimable now: on two complete 363-mixture proxy swarms with all predictions out of fold under repeated geometric blocking, a per-task ridge log-link on `log1p(epoch exposure)`, macro-averaged over 42 tasks, achieves lower macro-BPB RMSE than the incumbent summed-Huber OLMix law fit to the macro target, with corrected 95% intervals excluding zero in both pools (dclm −0.178 [−0.240, −0.116]; hq −0.111 [−0.184, −0.038]), and lower mean held-fold selection regret (0.0179 vs 0.0707; 0.0051 vs 0.0733).

Not claimable: that the gain is attributable to epoch exposure specifically rather than to log-compression of a heavy-tailed weight design, to ridge regularization the incumbent lacks, or to 42-task ensembling; that the saturating shape is ruled in or out; that the inventory-permutation result generalizes past one draw; anything about fresh optima or endpoint validation. The report's closing sentence already says the last of these, and that restraint is the report's strongest feature.

**B1 — Are fresh/repeated identified well enough?** Split verdict, and the report should say so. Fresh: adequately — 45.6% residual SD after projecting out phase-1 exposure and state effects, effective rank 23.9/39. Repeated: no — 1.9% residual SD, effective rank 7.8/39. The repeated-damage block is very nearly a linear combination of phase-1 exposure and the prefix intercepts. Since the mechanism's distinctive prediction is precisely that *repeat damage rises as fresh material is exhausted*, the half of the model that carries the claim is the half this panel cannot estimate. Compounding it, `fresh_share` is bimodal, so the split acts as a near-binary switch rather than a graded quantity. This panel can speak to fresh-benefit attenuation; it cannot speak to repeat damage.

**B2 — Is the blend mechanistically meaningful?** On the evidence here, no — it reads as flexible interpolation. Two facts drive this, neither in the report. First, `state_exhaustion` *is* `blend_1p00`, and RMSE along the blend path declines smoothly to w=0.75 then reverses sharply at the endpoint; a mechanism that improves fit should not collapse exactly where it is applied purest. The natural reading is that any w<1 retains the well-conditioned cumulative component and the state term acts as a small stabilized perturbation. Second, the t-statistic is ~1.0 regardless of w, so the family is uniformly unresolvable on this panel. And w=0.75 was chosen post hoc from three values scored on the same folds. The report's refusal to promote is correct; its stated reason should be this, not the tied-abstention leg.

**B3 — Do the diagnostics support a modeling update now?** No update to the state-dependent head. What the diagnostics do point at, and the report leaves unremarked, is that the *state-independent* one-epoch threshold `constant_exhaustion` is the best-transferring and best-ranking head: best LOPO by 1.8× over cumulative, best Spearman, lowest decision regret. That is a coherent negative result for state dependence — the models that condition on prefix state transfer worst across prefixes. The honest caveat is that its decision advantage is mostly abstention, since tied is optimal everywhere, so it should be reported as a transfer and ranking result, not a decision result.

**B4 — Smallest next falsifiable step?** Two, in order of cost.

*Free, no new compute:* re-run the corrected contrast at leave-one-action-out instead of 5-fold. The Nadeau–Bengio penalty here is dominated by `n_test/n_train = 1/(K−1) = 0.25`, not by the `1/(K·R) = 0.04` term. At K=50, R=1 the factor drops to `1/50 + 1/49 ≈ 0.040`, shrinking the SE by ~2.7× and moving the blend contrast from |t| ≈ 0.95 to roughly 2.5 if the mean difference is stable. That single re-analysis decides whether this panel already contains the answer or genuinely lacks power. It also removes the near-duplicate-repeat problem in `action_fold_ids` entirely.

*Cheapest new data, no semantic partition:* add phase-1 actions selected to hold cumulative per-bucket exposure `prefix + phase1` fixed while varying the split `min(phase1, max(1−prefix, 0))`. Under cumulative exposure such pairs are exchangeable by construction; under state-dependent exhaustion they are not, so a paired contrast over them is a direct falsification test with no bucket semantics involved. The design objective is exactly the quantity `identification()` already computes — choose actions maximizing repeated-exposure residual variance orthogonal to (phase-1 exposure, prefix intercepts), which currently sits at 0.019 and is the binding constraint. This is a small optimization over the existing exposure matrices, and the resulting head needs only the two blocks already implemented.

## 5. Verdict

Both reports reach conclusions I agree with, and both are unusually restrained — A explicitly declines to claim validation, B explicitly declines to promote. The defects below do not reverse either conclusion. I am blocking on them anyway because both are misstatements of what the evidence shows that are baked into machine-readable gate artifacts (`promotion_gate.json`, `mechanism_gate.json`) that will outlive the prose and be cited at face value, and both are cheap to fix without re-running either benchmark.

BLOCK

**Minimum required corrections:**

1. **A — fix the inventory-scramble claim.** `linear_epoch_log_link_minus_dsp_permuted_inventory` compares a `log1p`/free-sign head against a saturating/nonnegative head with a 4× larger shape grid, so it does not isolate inventory.
