## Blockers

**B1. The finalist claim for the bounded link is contradicted by the finalist artifacts, which now include `@link_by_cv`.**
Report §5 lines 150–152: "Finalist: … best of every finalist on six of eight cells and tied on the other two"; §0 line 19–20: "the one that wins at Certify and in the five-repeat finalist on every tabular cell". `finalist/aggregate_repeat_metrics.csv` contains five-repeat rows for `weibull_softplus_unscaled@link_by_cv` (the Codex review found zero such rows at 21:38–22:15; they were added afterward, file mtime 22:10:47). Numerically, link_by_cv leads the bounded link on 5 of 8 cells (60M U 0.005834 vs 0.005850, 60M T9 tie, 300M U 0.003897 vs 0.004011, 300M T9 0.011268 vs 0.011634, Delphi U 0.007384 vs 0.007505, dclm 0.101742 vs 0.102378) and the bounded link leads on Delphi T9 (0.026026 vs 0.026291) and high quality (0.064370 vs 0.065155). "Best of every finalist on six of eight" is therefore false against the artifact set; the bounded link is best on ~3 cells and within-repeat-SD behind on 5. This directly bears on "anything that would change which model the round should recommend": the finalist, like Certify, no longer separates the two, and the leader on the 39-bucket cells is the CV-selected link, not the bounded link.

**B2. The frozen promotion decision disagrees with the tiers the round actually ran, and the report does not disclose it.**
`screen/ablation_promotions.csv` records `promoted_to_certify=False`, reason "no interval excludes zero", for `@log_deficit_bounded_link`, `@link_by_cv`, `@refined_shape`, `@refined_bounded`, `@wide_grid_refined`, `@wide_grid_refined_bounded`, `@ablation_prior`, `@shared_shape_*`, `@huber_head`, `@wide_grid`, and both interaction entries. Yet the bounded link, link_by_cv, the refined variants, and the prior all appear in the top-level Certify tables, and two of them in the finalist, and §5 presents their Certify results as the round's headline. The harness has no promotion gate (nothing reads `ablation_promotions.csv` to select the Certify model list — `main()` at benchmark_single_phase_observatory_20260902.py:2647–2691 fits whatever `--models` says). These entries were advanced to Certify by operator choice on Screen tabular results, bypassing the frozen rule. That is exactly the "selection on Screen" the protocol was built to mark (§13 of the round-1 report), and it is unmarked here. The Certify/finalist replication of the tabular gain is real evidence, but the reader cannot see from the report that the promotion artifact says these entries were *not* promoted.

## Findings

**F1 (claim 1 — search budget).** The Screen nulls are budget-confounded for three mechanism classes, and only two controls exist. `@wide_grid` is 360 shapes vs the successor's 168 (registry:1404–1411) with no matched-budget control; `@link_by_cv` doubles inner-CV evaluations (models:1108, 1680 vs 840) with no matched-budget control; `@refined_*` adds up to 80 Nelder-Mead evaluations (models:87, 1025) with no control. Controls exist only for quality-axis pooling (`shuffled_quality`, registry:1813) and the prior (`scrambled_prior`, registry:1838). The plan's "matched ablations and controls" framing therefore does not hold for the wide grid, the CV-selected link, or the refinement entries, and the report's §3 readings for those rows are confounded with capacity. (The quality control is valid — models:713–717 permutes labels among labeled buckets keeping the pooling capacity; the prior control is valid — registry:1470–1471 permutes multipliers within metric, preserving the count of shrunk buckets.)

**F2 (claim 1 — Holm family).** `with_holm_correction` (benchmark:1409–1417) corrects within whatever rows are present in the report frame, one family per (comparator kind, metric) — the ~87 parent×rmse tests include all round-1 ablation contrasts. The family choice is material: `@quality_both`'s p=0.0029 becomes 0.25 in the shipped family but 0.052 in a round-2-only family of 18. The report discloses the definition and instability (lines 101, 186) but the Codex P2 request to freeze the family independently of report filtering was only disclosed, not implemented; the correction is conservative here, but the artifact does not record which family produced the quoted values. The correlated-unit caveat (38 units sharing panels/folds) is stated; the Holm values are not calibrated p-values either way.

**F3 (claim 2 — leakage).** None found. Pass 1 (`component_cv_table`, benchmark:887–913) fits the parent on `split.train` with inner folds ⊂ train; pass 2 selection (benchmark:928–931) aggregates only those inner-CV tables, and heads are refit on train rows only (benchmark:957–962). Test rows never enter shape or ridge selection.

**F4 (claim 2 — normalization).** Confirmed as reported: `panel.component_repeat_sd.get(...) or train_response_sd` (benchmark:928) mixes noise SDs (Delphi Table 9 only, from the static `noise_component_matrix.csv`) with response SDs everywhere else, so panel- and scale-level sharing weight components by noise-data availability. The null reading is confounded as disclosed (report lines 49, 203–204); the open item stands.

**F5 (claim 2 — shard keys).** The Codex P1 fix is partial. Shared shards now carry `shared_unit_hash` over the member set (benchmark:933–948) and are reused only on a match — that closes the Screen→Certify member-set bug. But `fit_path_hash` (benchmark:638–655) still omits `component_cv_table`, `fit_shared_unit`, and `shared_unit_key`, which Codex explicitly asked to hash. An edit to the shared-stage aggregation or normalization leaves existing shared shards accepted via the generation mechanism, because their protocol hash does not see the implementation. (For the current numbers this is moot — the shared entries were not promoted past Screen, and the pre-fix shards' predictions were unaffected by the fixes — but the protection gap remains.)

**F6 (claim 3 — prior mapping/transfer).** Mechanics verified by running `ablation_prior_table`: 376 matrix metrics; coverage is 7/7 Uncheatable components and 6/51 Table 9 components (the 6-of-51 disclosure is correct), so the prior acts on all Uncheatable heads and a handful of Table 9 heads; multipliers are ×10 where the two-sided deletion p ≥ 0.05 (registry:1469); the scramble permutes within metric. The 300M cells use outer-test responses (leak, disclosed; cross-fitting open) and the multipliers transfer untested to 60M/Delphi (disclosed). One further limitation is not stated: the ablation only exercises a mild ridge-multiplier change on 13 of 58 components, so the null is interpretable as "this multiplier scheme with this mapping is null", not as a test of significance-informed weighting in general.

**F7 (claim 4 — `_refine_shape`).** The core is correct for the round-2 entries: log-space keys `{rate, saturation_epochs, benefit_offset}` (models:88); hull bounds clip in decoded space (models:1003–1004); acceptance requires strictly better inner CV (models:1027); the only domain guards are `power<=0` and `threshold<0` (models:1015). Latent defects, unused by the round-2 entries: (a) the scoring spec is built from the *grid* shape once (models:1008), so a refined `floor_margin` would be scored with the stale spec while the fitted head uses the refined one — a mismatch for any `floor_margin` model refined in the future; (b) a negative refined `exponent` (power-benefit grids) is not guarded, so unbounded refinement could evaluate 1/E^|a| columns; (c) Nelder-Mead searches outside the hull and relies on decode-clipping, which flattens the objective and wastes evaluations but cannot accept a worse point. The unbounded collapses in the report match the artifacts (screen family summary: dense 2.52, matched 2,828.9; report's "power 1.6 or 0.002, rate 731" is consistent with the unclipped log-space decode). Reporting only the bounded variant is selection on the gate curves, and the report labels it a post-hoc repair correctly (lines 118–120).

**F8 (claim 5 — StarCoder gate).** All quoted counts verify exactly against `starcoder_all_tied_curves_canonical_dsp_20260902/metrics.csv`: in-sample CV-objective losses 33/33/35/28/32 with median ratios 1.56/1.91/1.29/1.06/1.84; argmin-regret worse on 12–19 curves; the withdrawn training-objective table's DSP rows match the page's `full_fit_rmse` on 0 of 45 curves (confirming Codex's P1); OOF worse-than-DSP counts are 5/15/6/10 for successor/bounded/refined_bounded/link_by_cv. Two residual issues: (a) the gate script itself (which joined these tables and computed the ratios) is not versioned in the tree — only its outputs are — so the like-for-like implementation cannot be re-audited; (b) "beats the benchmark's DSP on every family macro" holds only under the benchmark's 4-family macro: at the per-model-curve split in `starcoder_gate_round2_oof.csv` the successor is worse than DSP on the fixed-model 1b family (0.1030 vs 0.1012). The out-of-fold per-curve comparison is the right one for the generalization claim, and the report is honest that the in-sample gate is unmet.
