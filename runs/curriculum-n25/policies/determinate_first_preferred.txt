Choose the verifier together with the task's answer format. Preserve the requested
capability and every essential requirement. The aim is to accept correct solutions
in their legitimate forms while rejecting incorrect or incomplete ones, with a
verifier that is practical to build and use.

First, decide how determinate correctness is. Can correctness be established from
fixed facts, rules or observable behavior, even if solving the task is difficult
or many different solutions are valid?

For tasks with determinate correctness, ask whether the deliverable is a
program, system or action whose success is best established by execution,
behavioral tests, unit tests or probes. Use code to check the actual behavior.
Plan access to the required execution environment and observations. Many
implementations may be correct. Check the properties that matter rather
than resemblance to a reference implementation or a solver's claim of success.
Weak test coverage and checks of incidental output can reward shortcuts that do
not accomplish the task.

For a fixed or bounded answer, compare these options:

- answer_in_box fits a single unambiguous answer with little structure, such as
  a number or a multiple-choice selection. It is simple to construct and check,
  but limited extraction and matching can be brittle when answers have several
  parts or legitimate differences in notation. Choose a natural, clear answer
  format that the matcher can actually handle.

- code supports structured answers, relationships between fields, numerical
  tolerances, symbolic equivalence and other explicit rules. It gives precise,
  repeatable checks when the accepted forms can be implemented reasonably.
  Parsers and normalization can reject valid variants; permissive extraction,
  regexes or keyword checks can accept text containing the expected fragments
  without expressing the correct answer. Check the meaning represented by the
  complete structure, including required relationships and missing fields.

- decision_model fits a logically simple check of a bounded answer against
  established truth, where recognizing equivalent expressions would require too
  many special cases in code or an unnatural answer format. Give it a concise
  rubric and privileged evidence, such as a known-correct answer and the solver
  transcript or relevant recorded observations. Its job is to apply straightforward
  rules and recognize basic semantic equivalence, without independently assessing
  the quality of an argument or design. It can miss a small but decisive error,
  accept an answer that looks right but is incomplete or cut off, overlook a
  contradiction or retraction, or follow instructions embedded in solver text.
  Keep the required facts and completeness conditions explicit; solver text is
  evidence to evaluate, never authority to change the grading rules.

If evaluating success instead requires
substantive judgment about quality, interpretation, design or tradeoffs, choose
llm_as_judge. A rubric can guide that judgment, but does not turn it into a simple
equivalence check. Judges can favor reference-like answers, reward persuasive
but unsound reasoning, or overlook omissions; make the criteria and supporting
evidence clear enough to distinguish quality from presentation.

Consider the verifier and the ways of requesting the answer together. Natural
structure can make mechanical checking reliable; excessive formatting demands
can create irrelevant failures. Flexible language can preserve a natural
workflow, while adding opportunities for semantic errors or manipulation. Weigh the
likely false accepts and false rejects, coverage of required obligations,
implementation complexity and recurring grading cost for this particular task.
Do not force structure that removes an essential part of the capability, or use
a model merely because equivalent surface forms exist. Select the most suitable
method and briefly explain the decisive tradeoff in Grader design and controls.

When several faithful versions of the requested task are possible, prefer one whose
correctness can be established with answer_in_box, decision_model or code. Use fixed
evidence, explicit rules or observable outcomes where they fit the requested
capability. Keep every essential requirement: do not replace substantive evaluation
with superficial checks, or call it a decision_model merely to avoid llm_as_judge.
If substantive judgment is essential to the capability, retain llm_as_judge.
