## Overall verdict

The new link is a real improvement over the 0.95 rule, but for a narrower reason than its construction suggests: it removes a demonstrably wrong floor (the 0.95 rule sits above the bank minimum on 21/51 Table-9 tasks, worst excess 0.096 BPB; summary.md:24) and replaces it with a floor the frozen bank cannot refute (κ=2 fails 4/51, κ=2.5 fails 3/51; summary.md:26-27). The per-task inner-CV κ search, the response-space NLS, and the identity fallback are individually reasonable pieces, but the pipeline currently *interprets* the κ argmin as evidence about the floor at an out-of-support optimum, which it is not, and the identity fallback — not the κ search — is doing most of the safety work on the tasks where the swarm never moved. My main recommendations: switch the selection criterion from the profile *minimum* to the profile *upper band* (the permissive side), treat the bank gate as the primary floor evidence rather than decoration, and decide the WSPU/0.95/fitted-floor question with a scoring shootout on the frozen bank (Q6), not on inner CV.

## (1) Is per-task κ by inner CV defensible for extrapolating to the optimum?

**Not as evidence about the floor at the optimum.** Inner CV estimates the model's interpolation error over the swarm's observed deficit range; the floor is the asymptote of `y = φ + exp(η)` as η → −∞, which no training or validation row approaches (every row's deficit is ≥ `gap` by construction, since `gap = anchor − min(response)`, M:393-394). The CV profile's interior minimum at κ≈3 for `github_cpp` (0.0162 vs 0.0189 identity, 0.0230 for the 0.95 link) is a statement about the *curvature* of the link across the observed deficit band — how fast `exp(η)` must bend to track the swarm — not about where the response levels off beyond it. κ is identified purely from second-order curvature (the level is absorbed by the free intercept `c`, see (4)); curvature inside the swarm is exactly the quantity that is *not* transported to the asymptote.

The bank gives you an external read on the same quantity, and it disagrees with the CV read: bank empirical κ for code is median 1.37 [1.27, 1.56] (summary.md:33) while inner CV picks ≈3 — a 2× gap. So the two data sources that speak to the floor point at different places, and the one that actually visits the region (the bank) is the one being ignored in selection. The direction of the disagreement is safe (CV picks a *lower*, more permissive floor), but "safe direction" is doing the work here, not statistical justification.

There is also a structural point that cuts the other way: the predicted optimum is `floor + exp(c + X*β)` with `X*` the optimizer's endpoint, and the optimizer's ability to push `exp(η)` down is set by the Weibull asymptote and harm quadratic, not by κ. κ only matters when the model predicts deficits beyond the swarm range — i.e., exactly when the floor is the dominant term and the data have said nothing about it. Choosing κ by interior CV then is choosing the value of the most extrapolation-sensitive parameter by a criterion that never touches the extrapolation regime.

What the CV κ search *is* defensible for: (a) as a curvature/regularization choice that is de-biased by the outer nested protocol (5 outer mixture-blocked folds, benchmark_single_phase_observatory_20260902.py:88, 475-503), because the whole per-fold pipeline including the κ search is refit in each outer fold; (b) as a *lower bound* on permissiveness — because the cost function is asymmetric (a too-high floor demonstrably capped real gains: 11/51 tasks, 7–9% below the swarm minimum; handoff 8e), you want the κ that CV says is "too low to matter anymore", not the argmin. My recommendation: take the largest κ in [1,8] whose profile is within one inner-fold SE of the minimum (a one-SE-rule in the direction of permissiveness), or a fixed κ=3 with the identity fallback. That turns the CV from a biased point estimate into a safety margin, which is the only role it can honestly play.

## (2) Response-space NLS vs the log-deficit objective

**Response space is the right objective under your stated noise model.** If run noise is homoscedastic in BPB, minimizing Σ(y − φ − exp(η))² (M:510-514) is the correct Gaussian likelihood; the log-deficit alternative minimizes Σ(log(y−φ) − η)², which reweights residuals by 1/deficit — it puts the most weight exactly on the rows closest to the floor, i.e., the smallest deficits, which are the noisiest in relative terms and the least reliable guide to the asymptote. That reweighting is plausibly *why* the 0.95-link fit behaved badly in the floor region, so moving the refit to BPB space is the right fix, not just a cosmetic one. The ridge rows (M:502-503, 514) and the κ prior row (M:499, 514) are also in BPB units, so the objective is unit-consistent end to end.

Caveats, in order of importance:

- **The two stages use different objectives.** Shape and ridge are selected by the base grid under `KAPPA_FLOOR` in *log-deficit* space (R:1547, M:1357 via `_cv_rmse` → `fit_head` with the log link), and only then is κ searched under the response-space fit (M:1421-1436). The shape that wins log-space CV at κ=2.5 need not win response-space CV at the fitted κ; you're not optimizing one objective, you're cascading two. Acceptable as an approximation, but it should be documented as such, and the profile's flatness on math tasks is partly a symptom of this mismatch.
- **Non-convexity.** The response-space objective is nonlinear in β (through `exp`), unlike the convex log-space NNLS. The warm start (M:497, 529) mitigates this, and it is a good warm start (the log-space minimizer is the natural linearization point), but the fit can still terminate in a poor local mode on flat tasks, which biases the κ search *against* the fitted-floor link — a conservative bias, but one that inflates the identity win rate for the wrong reason. `solution.success` is never inspected (see (4)).
- The identity comparison is apples-to-apples (both scored as BPB RMSE over the same folds, M:1440-1445), which is correct. One refinement if you ever want it: the `repeat_sd` anchors could be used to *verify* homoscedasticity across tasks before committing to this objective, since the anchors file (R:1522-1529) already carries per-task SDs.

## (3) Is the proportional-anchor + swarm-gap parametrization sound?

Mostly yes, with four caveats, and I'd keep it but change the criterion and the fallback logic.

**What's sound:** anchoring on the proportional mixture's value and scaling by the swarm gap makes κ dimensionless and comparable across tasks; the anchor comes from denoised reliability data (multiple repeats, or an SNR fit) rather than a single panel row (build_delphi_floor_anchors_20260907.py:28-52); and the noise margin (M:402, 504) handles the degenerate case where the swarm never moved the task. The two-piece rule `max(κ·gap, 3·noise_sd)` is the right shape for that degenerate case.

**Caveats:**

- **Anchor mismatch between the check and the model.** The check computes `prop` from the panel's single proportional *row* (C:89-90), while the model uses the reliability-derived proportional *mean* (R:1552, anchors.csv). For tasks with small gaps, the κ_bank reported by the check is on a different scale than the fitted κ — for Uncheatable the difference is repeat noise of a few 1e-3 BPB against gaps that are sometimes smaller than that. The check should read the same `anchors.csv`.
- **The `gap ≤ 0` fallback mixes units** (M:395-397): `std(response)` vs `1e-3·|anchor|` are different scales and switch κ's interpretation silently. Rare, but it should be a flagged event.
- **The margin branch makes κ inert.** When `κ·gap < 3·noise_sd`, the floor doesn't depend on κ, the Jacobian column is zero (M:522), and the search objective is constant in κ — so the per-task "κ = 2.5" reported for flat tasks is the prior doing nothing, not a data-driven value. The identity fallback is what actually saves these tasks (AO3/ARC are the κ_bank up to 6.6 cases, summary.md:36, C:101). That's fine, but it means the fitted-floor mechanism's entire empirical content lives in the *moved* tasks (code), where its CV-selected κ and the bank's κ disagree 2×.
- **The 3·noise_sd constant is arbitrary** and the noise estimates come from two different estimators (SNR fit for Table-9, raw repeat SD for Uncheatable; build_delphi_floor_anchors_20260907.py:28-52). Not wrong, but worth a sensitivity run at 2/3/4 SDs since it's the binding term for the flat tasks.

**Alternatives, ranked by what I'd actually do:**

1. **Profile upper-band selection (cheap, do this first).** Keep anchor/gap, but select κ as the largest value within ~1 inner-fold SE of the profile minimum. This is the permissiveness-safety rule, kills the min-of-noise selection bias, and is one line in `FittedFloorModel.fit`.
2. **Task-family shrinkage rather than per-task argmin.** The registry already has the scaffolding (`floor_task_group`, R:1532-1542; `FITTED_FLOOR_GROUP_KAPPAS`, R:1519) but the group entry is currently a placeholder pinning κ=2.5 for everyone (R:1555-1558) — as written it will not test anything the prior-kappa variant doesn't. A real hierarchical version would put `log κ_t ~ N(log κ_f, τ)` with family-level hyperparameters estimated on the per-task *profiles* (not the argmins), which is exactly where the code-family's 1.4-vs-3 tension would be visible. Fill the group dict from per-task profile upper-band medians before running the group entry, and don't report it as a "group" result until then.
3. **Evaluation-text entropy: a gate on the predicted optimum, not a floor.** Entropy is a lower bound on *any* model's BPB on that distribution, so it bounds the surrogate's predicted optimum from below, not the floor φ from above. It's worth computing once as a sanity check ("is the predicted optimum physically impossible?") but it cannot discriminate floor rules, and at 3e18 the optimum will be far above it.
4. **Next-rung proportional value: only a weak aggregate gate.** It's one number per target, not per task, and it describes a different mixture's trajectory. The check already uses it correctly (C:159-164: the κ-2 aggregate floor stays above the next rung's proportional, which is the right direction for that comparison). The stronger version, if a next-rung *swarm* exists, is the next rung's per-task swarm minima — that would give empirical κs at the next rung directly and is the best anchor you could ask for.
5. **Bank-min-relative floor (φ_t ≤ bank_min_t − margin) — only for the deployed fit, never for selection.** It's the most defensible data-driven floor, but it consumes the held-out bank, so it must come *after* the Q6 decision and forfeits the bank as an honest validation set. I'd avoid it unless you're willing to accept a new validation stage.

## (4) Bugs and numerical risks in `_fitted_floor_solve` and `FittedFloorModel`

1. **`least_squares` success is never checked** (M:537-548). `solution.x` is returned whether trf converged, stalled at the kink, or hit `max_nfev=400`. A silently unconverged NLS feeds coefficients and the floor into both the CV scores and the final head. This is the one genuine *bug* in the review scope: assert `solution.success`, or at minimum record `solution.optimality`/`solution.nfev` in diagnostics and gate on it.
2. **The `max()` kink** (M:512, 522). Both the residual and the analytic Jacobian are non-smooth exactly at `κ·gap = 3·noise_sd`; the Jacobian is a correct subgradient away from the kink but trf assumes smoothness and can oscillate or stall there. In the per-fold κ search the kink is at a fixed κ so it's mostly harmless; in the free-κ fit (the unit test path, M:437-447) it's a real convergence hazard — the test only checks the endpoint, not success.
3. **The prior does not do what the comment claims.** M:92-94 says the prior "errs low: a floor too high blocks gains the optimizer never proposes", but the implemented prior is a single residual row `(log κ − log 2.5)·residual_sd/0.5` (M:499, 514) against 280 data rows: at κ=8 it contributes ≈2.3 residual SDs, which 280 rows outweigh trivially. Within [1, 8] the prior is essentially flat and provides no protection against a too-high floor. Either drop the claim or strengthen the prior (or use the profile-band rule from (3), which implements the same intent honestly).
4. **Ridge and prior scales are frozen at the warm start.** `deficit_scale` (M:502-503) uses the *prior-κ* mean deficit, but the ridge's effective strength in BPB units varies with κ (at κ=1 deficits shrink and the ridge is effectively stronger than in log space). The κ search therefore minimizes an objective whose ridge penalty drifts with the parameter being searched. Mild, and it biases toward larger κ, but it's an avoidable inconsistency — recompute `deficit_scale` at the fitted floor per evaluation if you care about κ precision.
5. **Pinned-bounds hair width** (M:532-533). With bounds `(κ, κ)` widened by 1e-6 in log space, the per-fold NLS can return a κ that differs slightly from the search κ; diagnostics record the *search* κ (M:1456) while `head.floor` uses the *fitted* κ (M:548). Currently negligible (1e-6 in log space), but the two sources of truth will eventually diverge in someone's downstream code — record `head.kappa` in diagnostics instead.
6. **Identifiability of κ vs the intercept `c`.** For rows near the swarm minimum, `y_min ≈ anchor − κ·gap + exp(η_min)` — raising κ is exactly compensated by raising `c`. κ is identified *only* through the curvature of the link across the observed deficit range, so when `gap` is small relative to noise the profile is flat and the fit is a ridge in (log κ, c) space. This is not a bug — it's the mathematical version of the answer to (1) — but it means the reported point κ has no meaningful SE, and the profile (or the band rule) is the only honest summary.
7. **`_cv_rmse` returns `inf` for non-finite predictions** (M:1075-1077), and that function is the `minimize_scalar` objective (M:1425-1428). A single inf evaluation can derail golden-section on a bounded interval (it can't bracket through an inf). Low probability given the `LINK_CLIP` clipping (M:513), but guard it with a large finite penalty.
8. **DOF accounting** (M:1461-1462, 1476-1477): κ is charged +1 dof even when identity wins and κ was never used in the deployed head. Defensible as a selection cost, but it's inconsistent with the code's own logic elsewhere; if any downstream IC/penalty uses `fitted_dof`, identity tasks are over-charged.
9. **Registry silent fallback** (R:1552): a component missing from `anchors.csv` silently becomes median-anchored with `noise_sd=0` — no noise margin, different anchor semantics, no warning. Against the project's fail-fast convention, this should raise or at least log; `build_delphi_floor_anchors` asserts the file has 58 rows (build script:54-55) but nothing asserts every registry component hits it.
10. **The cap is applied to the fitted-floor link in CV but not to identity** (M:602-603, 425-428). During the κ search, validation predictions are capped at the fold's max training log-deficit + 0.5, while the identity competitor is uncapped. Both are the models as deployed, so the comparison is defensible, but part of the κ argmin may reflect cap behavior rather than floor curvature. Worth one sentence in the note.
11. **The group entry is currently a κ=2.5 prior variant**, not a group fit (R:1519, 1555-1558). Don't run or report it as `@fitted_floor_link_group` until the dict is filled, or the ablation table will contain a duplicate of the prior-kappa variant under a misleading name.

## (5) Selection bias in the aggregate across 51 tasks

**Yes, two layers of it, and it's all in the *reported* scores, not the deployed predictions.** Per task, the reported `inner_cv_rmse = min(link_scores.values())` (M:1453) takes (a) the minimum over the κ profile — a noisy CV surface with only 3 mixture-blocked inner folds of ~93 rows each (benchmark_single_phase_observatory_20260902.py:89, 496-501) — and (b) the minimum over the two links. The expected min of two (or 24) correlated noisy estimates is below the true error by roughly the fold-noise SD; summed over 51 tasks with selection happening per task, the inner-CV aggregate is systematically optimistic, and any cross-model comparison that reads `inner_cv_rmse` from the diagnostics inherits it.

The *deployed* aggregate is not biased in expectation, because the outer protocol refits the entire pipeline — base grid, κ search, link choice — inside every outer fold and scores out-of-fold (the fits at M:1420-1446 run per outer split). So:

- **Estimate the optimism the way the benchmark already does:** the `score_delphi_selection_20260906.py` protocol's regret@1 / rank / optimism / RMSE columns on the frozen bank, with the existing source-block bootstraps. That optimism (predicted-at-argmin minus measured-at-argmin) is the right estimand — it is optimism *conditional on the optimizer's region*, which is what you care about, and the handoff shows it is exactly where the 0.95 link failed (optimism −0.015 vs WSPU's +0.070 on Table-9; handoff §1).
- **Never report the min-of-links inner CV as a performance number.** Keep it purely as the selection statistic, and report `selection_inner_cv_rmse` (the base grid's own score, M:1454) alongside, as the code already stores it.
- **The fair identity competitor is the standalone WSPU model, not identity-at-fitted-floor-shape.** Shape and ridge were selected under the log-deficit link at κ=2.5 (R:1547), so the identity arm of this model is a handicapped WSPU. For the "should this replace WSPU" question, compare the fitted-floor pipeline's outer OOF against the existing `weibull_softplus_unscaled` entry's outer OOF, not against the internal `identity_score`.
- One more channel worth remembering: the bank coordinates are themselves a *selected* set (optima of earlier campaigns, run_count ≥ 1, sources in `*_bank_labels.csv`), so bank-scored optimism estimates are valid only for the near-frontier region — which is the region where the 11-task floor failure lives, so it's the right region, but it will not tell you anything about the aggregate's behavior on arbitrary mixtures.

## (6) Cleanest frozen-bank experiment to decide floor-vs-WSPU-vs-0.95

The bank can't certify a floor, but it *can* certify the thing the floor is for: **the decision the optimizer would have made, and the value it would have gotten.** The experiment is a scoring shootout over the frozen bank (408 Uncheatable + 247 Table-9 coordinates, measured means in `*_bank_labels.csv`; per-coordinate per-component measurements in `heldout_coordinate_components.csv`), using only fold −1 fits and predictions — no new launches, no new fitting code:

**Candidates:** (a) WSPU identity (incumbent), (b) bounded log-deficit 0.95 (incumbent), (c) `@fitted_floor_link` as implemented, (d) the profile-upper-band variant from (3) — this is the variant I'd bet on, and it must be a separate registry entry so the comparison isn't confounded with the criterion change, (e) `@fitted_floor_link_group` only after its dict is filled.

**Scores, per target, on the bank:**
1. **Offline optimizer score:** rank all bank coordinates by each candidate's predicted aggregate and read off the measured aggregate of the argmin (regret@1), plus best-of-5 and rank — the existing `score_delphi_selection_20260906.py` protocol does exactly this, so run `evaluate_delphi_link_selection_20260906.py --methods <ids>` and diff. Bootstrap SEs over source blocks (the bank labels carry `sources`).
2. **Floor-region calibration:** per task, restrict to bank rows in the bottom decile of *measured* BPB and report RMSE and mean bias of each candidate there. This is the only score that directly measures what the floor link exists to fix — the region below the swarm minimum where the 0.95 link went wrong on 11/51 tasks. The fitted-floor candidate must beat both incumbents here by more than the block-bootstrap SE; if it doesn't, the link is not buying what it claims regardless of its overall RMSE.
3. **Validity gate:** the existing `check_delphi_link_floors_20260907.py --diagnostics <fit_diagnostics.csv> --method <id>` — zero tasks with a floor above `bank_min`. Keep it as a gate (never a scorer), and fix the anchor source mismatch noted in (3) first.
4. **Band ordering:** sign accuracy restricted to the top-30 band (the known failure region, handoff §10c). Replacing the link must not degrade ordering where ordering is already at chance — a floor that improves the aggregate RMSE while worsening near-frontier ordering would be a paper liability, and this catches it.

**Decision rule:** replace the 0.95 link (and WSPU) only if (c) wins score 2 beyond the SE *and* score 1 does not worsen beyond the SE *and* gate 3 passes *and* score 4 does not regress. Report all four regardless; a mixed result (better floor calibration, same regret) is itself the publishable finding.

**Two honest limitations to state in the writeup:** (i) the bank's minima are interior points of a *selected* region, so score 2 validates the floor only down to the best-known points — the region the optimizer will actually propose beyond the bank (e.g., the factorial's 1.5×/2× extrapolations, handoff §8f) is still uncovered, which is why the floor should be paired with a permissive selection rule (profile upper band) rather than a certified estimate; (ii) the already-measured validation runs — the link's Table-9 cap-8 optimum below the swarm minimum on 28/51 tasks — remain the only true floor-region measurements, and any promotion decision should be cross-checked against them before the paper claims the floor is "right" rather than merely "not yet refuted".
