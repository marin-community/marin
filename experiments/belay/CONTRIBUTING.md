# Contributing to Marin Belay

Marin Belay tests pretraining ideas against the official recipe and records every result. You post an idea; a
maintainer approves it; Belay's agent implements it behind a default-off flag, runs it, and posts the verdict.
Every experiment is compared to the official recipe unless its Track says otherwise.

**Links:** recipe branch [`marin_belay_v1_recipe`](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/tree/marin_belay_v1_recipe)

## Flow

1. **Check fit with the recipe.** Read `model.py` (yourself or with an agent) and search the registry for earlier
   attempts. Decide whether the idea replaces, adds to or adapts an existing feature, and how it scales with model
   size.

2. **Post it on #9514**, one idea per comment, with a `Track:` line (default `speedrun`). One idea is one change
   judged against one control; several values of one setting (up to 4) count as one idea. Post combinations of
   several changes as separate ideas, and a follow-up step (such as tuning the winner) as a new comment once the
   first result is in. Plain-text ideas are preferred. You can also link a paper, a GitHub repo, or a branch that applies the idea directly to the Belay
   architecture. Ideas for the scaling ladder should say how they scale with model size (e.g. "width 0.5·d", "every
   4th layer"), so each rung gets a consistent version rather than a constant tuned at one size.

   ```
   Track: speedrun
   Replace the sigmoid router scoring with sqrt-softplus on every MoE layer.
   ```

   | Track | Use it for |
   |---|---|
   | `speedrun` (default) | First test: a short run of the recipe at the smallest size. It needn't win: it checks the idea integrates cleanly and shows promise. |
   | `vanilla-<rung>` / `moe-<rung>` | Radical ideas: look for signs of life against a plain dense model or a basic MoE first. The rung is the model width, `d512`, `d768`, `d1024` or `d1280` (e.g. `moe-d768`). |
   | `hero-mfu` | Ideas that change speed or memory: step time at hero scale. Run by a maintainer. |
   | `ladder` | After a promising speedrun: every recipe rung, to see whether the gain holds with scale. |
   | `custom` | A study answered by analysis, with no win/loss verdict. It can involve generating plots from checkpoint weights. |

3. **Follow along on Status** (`…/#status/X####` once it has a registry id). Its row links the **code commit** (check
   the diff matches your intent), the **W&B runs** (variant vs. same-commit control), and the agent's plan and verdict.
   Results are single seed; treat anything near the noise band as weak evidence.

4. **Run the ladder only after a promising speedrun.** The ladder trains at every rung, up to the largest, and
   costs many times the compute of a speedrun, so it is reserved for ideas with initial evidence. Once the speedrun
   looks promising (a win isn't required), post a new comment with `Track: ladder` that names the idea's
   registry id, so the ladder reuses the same implementation. A ladder without a `hero-mfu` result is provisional;
   ask for `hero-mfu` too if the idea changes step time.

5. **Merge.** Once an idea holds up on `speedrun`, `hero-mfu` and the `ladder`, a maintainer can merge it into the
   official recipe. Depending on the magnitude of the change, further tests may be run first, such as larger
   scaling ladder rungs. Later ideas are compared against the improved recipe.

6. **If the implementation missed your intent, post a new idea** naming the old registry id and what to change.

## Automated harnesses

Autoresearch-style harnesses can take part in the same flow. Submit ideas as comments on #9514, and read progress
from the Status page's JSON
([`api/public/state`](https://public.applets.marina.oa.dev/a/33801d46-e280-46f9-b994-2c78603cdf15/api/public/state):
ideas, runs and the agent's verdicts) or from the registry
([`api/public/results`](https://public.applets.marina.oa.dev/a/33801d46-e280-46f9-b994-2c78603cdf15/api/public/results),
or the YAML on `marin_belay_v1_registry`).

- Every idea still needs a maintainer's approval; automation doesn't skip review.
- Keep at most 5 ideas pending at a time per harness, and wait for verdicts before submitting follow-ups.
- Say in the comment that it was generated by a harness, and include its reasoning: what it read, and what prior
  results motivated it.
- Check the registry before submitting, so you don't repeat an idea already tested on the same baseline.
