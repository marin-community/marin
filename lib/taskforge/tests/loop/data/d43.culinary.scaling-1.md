## Task

The solver sees the following request (canonical text lives in `task/solver_prompt.md`; numbers below are the fixed instance data).

**Scenario.** You are the production planner for a hotel banquet kitchen. The executive chef hands you the recipe card for a plated dinner currently written for **60 covers**, plus a change order from the event coordinator: the event is now **145 covers**, same menu, same per-plate specs. Procurement also flags two changes: the green beans will come from a cheaper supplier grade with a higher trim loss, and the protein will be finished sous-vide instead of pan-roasted, changing its cooking loss.

**Recipe card, 60 covers (all quantities as-purchased / as-prepared unless noted):**

| Component | Card quantity (60 covers) | Loss factors on the card | Portion spec (per plate) | Purchasing unit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Protein — airline chicken breast, skin-on | 11.86 kg AP | trim loss 8% (skin/sinew); cooking loss 12% (pan-roast) | 160 g cooked | kg |
| Vegetable — green beans | 7.32 kg AP | trim loss 18% (stem/tip); blanching loss 10% | 90 g cooked | 5-kg cases |
| Starch — dry orzo | 2.75 kg | none (cooked to order, no net yield loss stated) | — | kg |
| Sauce — red-wine demi mounted with cream | demi-glace 3.6 L pre-reduction; pouring cream 1.8 L | demi reduces 25% by volume; cream is mounted after reduction and is not reduced; finished sauce is 3:2 reduced demi : cream | 75 mL finished sauce | L (per sauce ingredient) |
| Garnish — confit cherry tomatoes | 180 pieces (3 per plate) | none | 3 pieces | dozens |

**Change order for 145 covers:** same portion specs and per-plate amounts for every component; green bean trim loss rises to **22%** (new supplier grade); protein cooking loss falls to **9%** (sous-vide finish). All other loss factors are unchanged.

**Deliverables:**
1. A short worksheet showing, for the protein and the vegetable, the full chain — total cooked requirement → pre-cook edible portion (EP) → as-purchased (AP) weight — and a yield check proving that starting from your AP quantities and applying the loss factors recovers 160 g cooked protein and 90 g cooked vegetable per plate at 145 covers.
2. A machine-readable results block, delimited by the exact marker lines `===RESULTS===` and `===END===`, containing one line per key below, in the form `KEY = number` (plain decimal, no thousands separators, no units in the value, value expressed in the unit named by the key):

```
PROTEIN_COOKED_KG, PROTEIN_EP_KG, PROTEIN_AP_KG,
VEG_COOKED_KG, VEG_EP_KG, VEG_AP_KG, VEG_CASES_5KG,
STARCH_KG, SAUCE_FINISHED_L, DEMI_REDUCED_L, DEMI_PREREDUCTION_L, CREAM_L,
GARNISH_PIECES, GARNISH_DOZENS, YIELD_PROTEIN_G_PER_PLATE, YIELD_VEG_G_PER_PLATE
```

**Constraints and stated conventions (given to the solver):**
- Portion specs and loss factors are authoritative; the 60-cover card quantities are reference data. Any correct method converges, but the numbers must satisfy the specs, not merely scale the card.
- Report every value to at least two decimal places or at least three significant figures, whichever is coarser-safe.
- VEG_CASES_5KG is the case *equivalent* of the AP weight to two decimals; do **not** round up to whole cases — purchase-order rounding is out of scope for this task.
- The sauce's 3:2 ratio applies to the *finished* (mounted) sauce; the 25% reduction applies only to the demi-glace.
- Stated loss percentages are losses at each stage (e.g., 22% trim loss means EP = AP × 0.78), not yields.

**What makes it difficult:** the answer requires chaining divisions in the correct direction with per-stage factors (cooked → EP → AP), correctly identifying *which* factors changed (protein cooking loss, vegetable trim loss) and which did not (protein trim, vegetable blanch loss, sauce reduction), and not applying losses to components that have none. Scaling the card linearly, using the old loss factors on the new yield, or adding loss percentages together all produce plausible-looking but wrong purchasing quantities. Unit conversions (g→kg, pieces→dozens, kg→5-kg cases, mL→L) are embedded throughout.

**How it exercises the capability:** yield-factor calculations and edible-portion/trim-loss chains (protein, vegetable), weight/volume/count conversions and purchasing-unit conversion (all components), ratio preservation (sauce 3:2), portion-size preservation (160 g / 90 g / 75 mL / 3 pieces). It respects the capability's excludes: every quantity, factor, ratio, and portion size is supplied; the solver only computes and documents — no prep timing, recipe creation, or quality judgment.

## Realism and workflow

**Who and why.** A banquet sous chef or catering production planner converting a recipe card into a purchasing and prep worksheet after an event's cover count changes. This is a routine, high-stakes foodservice workflow: order too little protein and plates fail the portion spec; order too much and the cost variance is flagged. Supplier-grade and cook-method changes altering yield factors are everyday realities (cheaper produce trims worse; sous-vide loses less moisture than pan-roasting), and the planner's job is exactly to recompute AP quantities rather than linearly scale the old order.

**Workflow reproduced.** Recipe card + change order → per-component requirement calculation → EP/AP chain with loss factors → conversion to purchasing units → yield check back to the portion spec. The solver's free-form worksheet mirrors the memo a chef would hand to procurement; the results block mirrors the line items procurement enters into the ordering system.

**Environment fit (no change from the planned slot).** The work is a pure multi-step numeric reasoning chain over fully supplied data — no tools, files, or processes are needed, so `reasoning` is correct. Solver and grader run separately: the solver submits a text response; the evaluator side runs the parser/checker as real code, so `code` verification needs nothing from the solver's environment. The portfolio's excluded combination (reasoning + simple) is respected: exact-match checking would either reject valid rounding conventions or accept coincidental trap values, so verification is executable with stated tolerances.

**Visible vs. evaluator-only.** The solver sees the scenario, card, change order, deliverable spec, and the exact key list. Evaluator-only: the answer key, tolerance values, weights, the aggregation formula, and all control responses. Nothing in the solver view reveals expected values, tolerances, or how close a trap answer is.

**Slot-specific limitation (acknowledged):** this slot cannot test tool use or artifact formatting; slots 2 and 3 of the portfolio cover those.

Also store per-key tolerances (below), weights, and tier assignments. *Acceptance check:* an independent recomputation script, written from the derivation strings only, reproduces every stored value bit-for-bit; additionally the 60-cover card is regenerated from the same specs with base factors (protein AP 11.86 kg, veg AP 7.32 kg, demi 3.6 L, cream 1.8 L, starch 2.75 kg, garnish 180) and matches the prompt within 0.01.

**Session B — write the solver prompt** (produces `task/solver_prompt.md`).
Assemble the canonical text from the Task section, including a worked example of one results-block line (`STARCH_KG = 6.65`) so the format is unambiguous. *Acceptance check:* the Session A consistency script validates every card number in the final prompt text; a formatting checklist review confirms all 16 key names appear verbatim and the authority statement (specs over card scaling) is present.

**Session C — write the grader** (produces `grader/check_response.py`).
Plain Python 3, no dependencies, runnable anywhere. Components:
- *Parser:* find the first `===RESULTS===` … `===END===` block (block extends to end of response if END is missing; tolerate trailing whitespace and CRLF). Within it, match lines of the form `KEY = value` (exact uppercase key, optional surrounding whitespace, `=` separator, optional whitespace; value: optional sign, digits, optional decimal part; no thousands separators, units, fractions, or scientific notation). Duplicate keys: last occurrence wins. Keys outside the list are ignored; missing keys fail.
- *Numeric check:* key passes iff |x − x*| ≤ max(abs_tol, 0.005·x*), with abs_tol = 0.02 for kg/L keys, 0.05 for VEG_CASES_5KG and GARNISH_DOZENS, 1 for GARNISH_PIECES, 1 g for the two YIELD keys.
- *Yield-consistency check:* YIELD_PROTEIN_G_PER_PLATE passes iff the stated value is within 1 g of 160 **and** within 1 g of the value recomputed from the solver's own PROTEIN_AP_KG (AP × 0.92 × 0.91 × 1000 / 145); same construction for vegetables (AP × 0.78 × 0.90 × 1000 / 145 vs 90). This makes an asserted-but-false check fail even when the asserted number is copied from the spec.
- *Aggregation:* raw = passed weight / 30, where weights are: Tier A (×3): PROTEIN_AP_KG, VEG_AP_KG, STARCH_KG, DEMI_PREREDUCTION_L, CREAM_L, GARNISH_DOZENS; Tier B (×1): PROTEIN_COOKED_KG, PROTEIN_EP_KG, VEG_COOKED_KG, VEG_EP_KG, VEG_CASES_5KG, SAUCE_FINISHED_L, DEMI_REDUCED_L, GARNISH_PIECES; Tier C (×2): the two YIELD keys. Let a = number of Tier-A keys passing. Multiplier m = 1.0 if a = 6, 0.75 if a = 5, 0.5 if a ≤ 4. Reward = raw × m; reward = 0 if no results block parses.
- *Selftest mode (`--selftest`):* unit tests for parser edge cases (CRLF, missing END, duplicates, non-numeric values, extra keys, values with units) and arithmetic spot checks.
*Acceptance check:* selftest passes; grading the Session D correct control returns exactly 1.0.

**Session D — author controls** (produces `grader/controls/*.md`).
Eight hand-written responses with pre-computed expected rewards (see Grader design). *Acceptance check:* running the grader over all eight reproduces every expected reward exactly; any mismatch blocks release.

**Dependency graph:** A → B (prompt needs frozen numbers) and A → C (grader needs key/tolerances/weights); B and C → D (controls need prompt format and grader); D is the release gate.

## Grader design and controls

**Observable success condition:** the response contains a parseable results block in which all six purchasing quantities (Tier A) are correct within tolerance, the intermediates are correct, and the yield checks are consistent with the solver's own AP values. Full pass = reward 1.0, which requires all Tier-A keys correct.

**Grader design.** Code verification: deterministic parsing plus numeric tolerance checks plus the recomputed yield-consistency checks, aggregated by the exact formula in Session C (raw × multiplier, 0 if unparseable). Normalization is fully specified there: case-sensitive exact keys, `=`-separated plain decimals, values in the unit named by the key, last-duplicate-wins, tolerances as stated. There is no judge component, so no rubric; all partial credit is arithmetic and pre-computable.

**Controls and exact expected rewards:**

| Control | Description | raw | Tier-A pass | Reward |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 correct, full precision | all 16 values exact | 30/30 = 1.000 | 6/6 | **1.00** |
| C2 correct, 2-dp rounded | every value rounded to 2 dp (e.g. 25.49, 27.71, 18.59, 3.72, 6.53, 6.65) | 1.000 | 6/6 | **1.00** |
| C3 naive card scaling | every card line × 145/60; per-plate asserted 160/90 | 18/30 = 0.600 | 4/6 | **0.30** |
| C4 additive losses | AP = cooked/(1 − trim − cook): protein 27.95, veg 19.19; EPs correct | 19/30 = 0.633 | 4/6 | **0.317** |
| C5 old loss factors | 12% cook / 18% trim applied to new yield: protein 28.66, veg 17.68 | 18/30 = 0.600 | 4/6 | **0.30** |
| C6 sauce reduction misapplied | 25% reduction applied to the whole finished sauce: cream 5.8 L, demi-reduced 8.7 L; everything else correct | 26/30 = 0.867 | 5/6 | **0.65** |
| C7 unit error | protein chain correct but PROTEIN_AP reported as 27711.4 (grams) | 25/30 = 0.833 | 5/6 | **0.625** |
| C8 missing block | correct prose worksheet, no results block | — | — | **0.00** |

C2 verifies tolerance generosity for legitimate rounding; C3–C5 verify the three headline traps are caught (naive protein AP 28.66 vs 27.71; old-factor 28.66; additive 27.95 — the tightest trap, 0.87% relative error against a 0.5% tolerance); C6 verifies the sauce-reduction trap (cream 5.8 vs 4.35); C7 verifies unit errors are caught rather than partially credited; C8 verifies the format requirement is load-bearing.

**Anti-shortcut controls.** Expected values are deliberately non-round, so guessing fails tolerance; the tolerance band is narrower than every trap's error (verified by C3–C6), so plausible-but-wrong chains cannot pass by luck. The yield keys are recomputed from the solver's own AP values, so copying "160"/"90" from the spec without a correct AP chain fails both the value and consistency conditions. Extra keys, duplicate keys, and unit-laden values are handled by specified parser rules and selftest cases.

**Prompt-injection controls.** The solver-visible content is entirely builder-authored instance data with no external documents, so there is no untrusted text to inject; the grader reads only the delimited block and ignores all other content, so instructions smuggled into prose outside the block have no effect.

**Privacy.** The answer key, tolerances, weights, aggregation formula, and all controls exist only in `grader/` and never appear in `task/solver_prompt.md`. The solver learns only its final reward, not per-key results, so trap margins cannot be probed.

## Risks and null conditions

**Risks and mitigations:**
1. *Additive-loss trap margin is the tightest* (protein: 0.87% error vs 0.5% tolerance, ~1.7× separation). Mitigation: C4 asserts separation at build time; if separation ever falls below 1.5×, widen the factor spread (e.g., protein trim 8%→10%, regenerating card and key in one edit) and re-run all controls. Abandon-if: after two iterations, legitimate 3-significant-figure rounding cannot be separated from the additive trap by ≥1.5×.
2. *Method ambiguity (card scaling vs spec-driven).* Mitigation: the prompt states that specs and factors are authoritative and the card is reference; the card is internally consistent with the base factors so both correct paths converge. Abandon-if: build-time pilot runs (three sample solver passes during Session D) show two legitimate interpretations yielding different accepted answers that the grader cannot both reward.
3. *Parser brittleness rejecting valid responses.* Mitigation: worked example line in the prompt, CRLF/duplicate/missing-END selftests, C2 rounding control. Abandon-if: a correctly formatted control fails to parse after the specified edge cases are implemented.
4. *Factor-semantics mismatch with industry convention* (references often publish EP/AP yield percentages rather than loss percentages). Mitigation: the prompt defines loss semantics explicitly ("22% trim loss means EP = AP × 0.78"); research only adjusts realism, not semantics. Abandon-if: research shows the chained AP→EP→cooked convention cannot be stated unambiguously — not expected, since the task fully defines its own factors.
5. *Unrealistic factors or purchasing units.* Mitigation: the two research entries; factors are task-supplied data, so this is cosmetic. Abandon-if: research shows a stated factor is off by an order of magnitude and cannot be corrected without breaking the trap margins.
6. *Tolerance unfairly tight for legitimate work.* Mitigation: C2 must return 1.00; any change to tolerances re-runs C3–C6 to confirm traps still fail.

**Data provenance and license.** All instance numbers are synthetic, builder-derived arithmetic documented in Session A; no datasets, tables, or benchmark items are copied. Research sources are used for range validation only, with facts cited and no content reproduced, so no license constraints attach to the artifacts.

**Split group.** This is a single task instance. If a variant family is later generated from the same card (different covers or factor changes), all variants derived from this card and this answer-key derivation stay in one train/eval split, since they share source data and solution structure.

**Contamination check.** Before release, web-search the distinctive values ("145 covers" with "27.71 kg" / "18.59 kg" / "36.25 dozen") to confirm no public culinary-math answer key matches this instance; the check is unverified until performed at build time. Expected values are freshly computed for this instance and not taken from any existing benchmark.

**Null conditions.** None anticipated: the arithmetic contract is closed, the grader is deterministic, and every trap has a pre-computed separation. The proposal should be abandoned (null) only if risk 1 or risk 2 hits its abandon-if condition after the stated iterations.
